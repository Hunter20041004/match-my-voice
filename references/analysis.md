# Analysis and calibration

## Evidence to capture

For each useful pattern, record its scope, evidence, and status: explicit preference, repeated observation, or tentative inference. Do not infer personality diagnoses or sensitive traits from writing.

Look at:
- Idea order: conclusion first, story first, question first; how reasons and examples connect.
- Vocabulary: familiar expressions, technical terms, regional variants, degree of formality.
- Stance: certainty, qualification, disagreement, how the writer presents personal judgment.
- Relationship: directness, politeness, humor, distance from the reader.
- Rhythm: clause connections, sentence variation, paragraph breaks, openings and endings.
- Context: which patterns persist and which shift between messages, applications, essays, or public posts.

## Language-specific adaptation

For Chinese, examine characters and clauses rather than assuming space-separated words. Attend to particles such as 「吧」「呢」, first-person framing, omitted subjects, conjunctions, full-width punctuation, and regional vocabulary. These are observation categories, not mandatory features. Traditional script alone does not establish a Taiwanese register.

For English, contractions, word choice, spelling variants, and punctuation may be useful if evidenced. Do not ask about English mechanics when analyzing another language. For mixed-language text, observe when and why switching occurs rather than translating it away.

For transcripts, punctuation may come from transcription software and fillers may reflect live planning. Confirm intended written style before treating these as deliberate habits. This skill matches verbal expression in text, not vocal pitch, timbre, accent, or an audio identity.

## Small meaningful check

When enough material exists, reserve a passage from profile building and compare a trial draft with that unseen example. Otherwise label the check as limited. Do not manufacture a holdout by splitting a tiny excerpt and then call the result reliable.

Check separately:
1. Did all source facts and qualifications survive?
2. Does the trial match evidenced patterns without exaggeration?
3. Is it appropriate for the requested audience?
4. What did the person actually correct or approve?

Do not score similarity numerically without a justified measurement method. The person's assessment is the primary calibration signal. One successful trial does not validate all languages or contexts.

## Avoid false generalization

- Fictional example: a person deletes a joke from a condolence message. Record a context-specific tone correction, not “never uses humor.”
- Fictional example: samples repeatedly use 「我覺得」. Capture first-person stance; do not prepend it to every paragraph.
- Fictional example: one profile favors concise conclusions and another favors narrative openings. Keep both; neither is the skill's default voice.
